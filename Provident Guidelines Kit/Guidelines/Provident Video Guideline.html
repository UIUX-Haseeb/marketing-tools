Foundations

The four color laws

“Navy dominates. Orange punctuates. Warm neutrals carry the breathing room.” The legacy teal (#1C6E8C) from the old template sheet is retired; the Literata italic accent word now inherits its headline color.

1 — No orange on blue. Ever.

Navy surfaces, panels, scrims and dark footage all count as blue. On them the orange budget is zero and the logo switches to the all-white lockup.

2 — White headings on navy.

Key headings and important overlays on navy are set in white. Support copy steps down to Mist, data labels to Stone.

3 — Blue text on light.

Deep Navy is the default text color wherever the ground is Paper or white: headlines, body and figures alike.

4 — Orange at dot scale only.

At most 10% of any design by area, 1–2% of visual emphasis: the logo dot plus one dot-sized accent per frame. Never text, never fills, never CTAs.

Logo & typography

The two video lockups

On Paper / white — navy + orange dot
On navy / footage — all white, no orange
  • Standard size 193 × 40 px on 1080-wide frames; closing frames may scale the logo up to ~480 px, centered.
  • Clear space: minimum 40 px (one logo height) on all sides. No type, QR or platform UI inside it.
  • The wordmark is never redrawn, recolored, outlined or skewed. Only the two lockups above exist on video.

Safe zones on vertical platforms

Working rule on a 1080 × 1920 master: keep 250 px top, 340 px bottom and 120 px right clear. Faces, headlines and the logo stay inside the middle 70% of frame.

ZoneWhat lives there
Top 250 pxProfile icon and platform chrome (Stories, Reels, TikTok)
DLD QR · top-rightReserved 250 × 250 px slot directly below the top clear zone, inside the icon-stack line; nothing else enters it
Bottom 340 pxCaptions, CTAs, comment box. Subtitles sit just above this line, above the repost icon
Right 120 pxLike / comment / share / repost icon stack
100 px marginsOn 1:1, 4:5, 3:4 and 16:9 formats, the margin grid applies on every edge

Build these as locked guide layers in the Premiere / After Effects master projects so editors never eyeball it.

House rules for every platform

Duration

The 45-second rule

All short-form content is capped at 40–45 seconds regardless of what the platform allows, because completion rate drives distribution on every algorithm. Exceptions: long-form YouTube and podcast (under 20 minutes).

Subtitles

Company font first

Google Sans Flex Medium is the default for burned-in subtitles: white, center-frame, above the repost icon. For creative edits, stylized and third-party fonts are allowed; keep them legible and inside the safe zones. YouTube long-form / podcast: separate SRT per language (EN/AR/RU), never burned in. YouTube Shorts still get burned-in subs.

Closing frame

Two lockups only

Allowed: fade in / fade out, and a smooth zoom toward the logo when needed. For creative aesthetics the closing logo may sit directly over the final shot, always as the all-white lockup over footage. No spins, no drastic transitions.

Thumbnails

Stills for the graphics team

Thumbnails are created by the graphics team, not the video team. From every video, deliver 1–3 clean frame options at full resolution: the agent or the unit, well lit and sharp. The stills carry no graphics, no subtitles, no burned-in UI; the graphics team builds the thumbnail on top.

Delivery

Production rules by format

Campaign videos

One master, many variants

Max 45 s. Deliver as a variant matrix of language, currency and hook (EN/AR/RU × AED/USD/EUR × 2–3 hooks): one master timeline; swap hook, VO and currency supers only. Shot inside the office by default. Cut on every line: one sentence, one shot.

Informative / educational

Office locations, line-by-line

Shot across multiple office locations (meeting rooms, lounge, balcony, desks) to vary the frame without leaving the building. Same 45 s cap, same line-by-line cut rhythm. Every number on screen is a super in the company type.

Long-form podcast

Under 20 minutes

16:9 master for YouTube with SRT subtitles per language. Cut 40–45 s vertical clips from every episode for Reels / TikTok / Shorts; clips get burned-in subs.

Stylized AI staging

One clean shot per room

One slow, continuous moving shot per room (gimbal push-in or lateral glide). Never cut between angles of the same room; inconsistent angles break AI generation. Lock exposure and white balance; no whip pans, no handheld shake.

Fast cuts / trend edits

Clean source footage only

Every source shot must be stabilized and high frame rate: 60 fps minimum, 120 fps if speed-ramping. Fast editing only works when the raw footage is clean. Brand frames and closing logos keep their own timing even inside a fast cut.

Outputs

The DLD QR code

Which code goes on? Every project has its own DLD permit QR; use the one for the project in the video. General Provident brand videos carry the Provident QR.

A wrong QR code, or no QR code, on a real-estate marketing video can result in a fine. The permit QR is a legal requirement, not a design choice. Treat it like the logo: locked position, locked size, on every property video.

RuleSpecification
PositionTop-right on every format: below the top clear zone on vertical (260 px from top, 120 px from the right edge); inside the 100 px margins on square and landscape
Size250 × 250 px on 1080-wide masters; scale proportionally on 4K exports
TreatmentOn its white plate with a clear quiet zone. Never animated, never semi-transparent, never behind graphics or subtitles
DurationFirst frame to last frame; the QR stays up until the video ends
VerificationScan-test the export on a phone before delivery; confirm it opens the correct project permit
ExceptionsFun / entertainment content and office-culture videos with no property marketing carry no QR
